== Governance, Risk, and Compliance (Security and compliance use case)

RHACM provides Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) functionality to ensure your clusters and applications do not drift from their original configurations.

=== Creating Policies in ACM

In RHACM, you build policies that are applied to managed clusters to govern their configurations. This reduces risk and ensures compliance.

. To create a policy, navigate to the *Governance* screen and click *Create policy*.
. We will build a simple policy to check for expiring certificates.
* **Name**: `policy-grc-cert`
* **Namespace**: `default`
. Click *NEXT*.
. For *Policy Templates*, click *Add policy template* and select *Certificate Management expiration*.
. Click *NEXT*.
. For *Placement*, click *New placement* and select `global` as the cluster set.
. Leave other settings as default, click *NEXT*, review, and then *SUBMIT*.

The policy will be created and applied to all clusters in the `global` ClusterSet. It will report a non-compliant status if it finds any certificate set to expire in less than the `minimumDuration`.

=== Deploying Policies with Policy Generator

The Policy Generator can be used to create configuration policies. We will deploy an application that generates two policies:
* `openshift-gitops-installed`: Informs if the OpenShift GitOps operator is installed.
* `kubeadmin-removed`: Informs if the `kubeadmin` user has been removed.

.Prerequisite: Ensure the `local-cluster` has the label `rhdp_usage=development`.

. In the ACM UI, navigate to *Applications*.
. Click *Create application*, select *Subscription*.
. **Name**: `policy-generator`
. **Namespace**: `policy-generator`
. Select the *GIT repository* type.
* **URL**: `https://github.com/levenhagen/demo-policygenerator.git`
* **Branch**: `main`
. Under deployment options, select *Deploy application resources only on clusters matching specified labels*.
* **Label**: `name`
* **Value**: `local-cluster`
. Click *Create*.
. After the application deploys, navigate to the *Governance* menu and click the *Policies* tab. You should see the two new policies.
. If a policy is not compliant (e.g., if GitOps was not installed), you can change its remediation action from `inform` to `enforce` by clicking the three-dots menu next to the policy. This will cause ACM to automatically remediate the violation.
